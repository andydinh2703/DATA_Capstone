Family Structure: How should we visualize it?

The data breaks down households with young children into Three types per location per year. Which view best serves your non-profit audience?

A

Stacked Area / Bar — Composition over time

Show what share of families with young kids are two-parent vs. single-mother vs. single-father, as a percentage. Emphasizes how the mix is shifting year to year.

B

Single-metric line chart — Two-parent % only

Simplify to one number: % of families with children under 6 that are two-parent households. Matches the existing tab style exactly — one line per location, easy to compare Geneva vs. Ontario vs. NYS.

C

Grouped bar chart — all three types side by side

For each available year, show bars for all three household types grouped by location. Good for seeing the full picture but visually busy — 3 locations × 3 types = 9 bars per year.

The family structure data is sparse — only 5 available years (2010, 2012–2015, 2017). Option B or C would show gaps; Option A handles them more gracefully as a stacked bar.
